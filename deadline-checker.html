How the California filing window works

California gives you a six-month window, not a single date. The window ends on the last day of the month you registered in, and opens on the first day of the month five months before that. File any time inside the window.

How often do I file?

  • Corporations (stock): every year, on Form SI-550
  • LLCs: every two years, on Form LLC-12
  • Nonprofit corporations: every two years, on Form SI-100

For the biennial filers, the years line up with your registration year. Register in an odd year and you file in odd years; register in an even year and you file in even years.

What if I just registered?

Your initial Statement of Information is due within 90 days of registration, regardless of the window above. After that first one, you fall into the regular cycle.

What actually happens if I'm late?

The Secretary of State sends a notice of delinquency giving you additional time to file. If you still don't file, the SOS refers it to the Franchise Tax Board, which assesses a penalty of $250 for most businesses and $50 for exempt organizations. The FTB collects it on the SOS's behalf — and only the SOS can waive it.

Beyond the money, continued failure to file can lead to suspension or forfeiture of your entity. A suspended entity loses the right to do business in California, can't defend or bring a lawsuit, and can lose the exclusive right to its own name.

Can I file it myself?

Yes. Filing is done at bizfile Online and the state fee is $25 for corporations and $20 for LLCs. The friction is the access step: since August 1, 2026 the state requires established User Access on your entity's record before an online Statement of Information can be submitted. If your business hasn't set that up, the state mails a PIN letter to your address on record — which takes about a week.
